Membership
Members fell from 887 in March to 728 in September.That count includes students, founders and suspended members. New sign-ups halved while cancellations held steady.
The April price increase cost about 35 members beyond normal attrition (9% of the 387 repriced) but raised their membership revenue by $16,900 (3.5%) over six months.It has paid off so far, narrowly. Retention is the risk: 59% of repriced members were still paying six months later against a normal 68%, and by September the monthly effect had turned negative (−$9,400).

What the April cut did to new clients

Actual new clientsModel: expected without the cut
New clients per month, all studios. Before April the blue line is the model's fit; from April it shows what the model expects had marketing continued.

The model follows new clients at each studio every month from January 2023 to September 2026. It allows for the time of year, each studio's opening surge, new studios opening nearby, and January's list price increase, then measures the step change when marketing was cut in April 2026.

New clients fell 30% below the expected level (likely range 21% to 39%), about 190 a month. Marketing fell by about $20,400 a month, so each lost new client corresponds to about $108 of marketing (likely range $74 to $181).

If instead the whole year-on-year drop in new clients (231 a month) is put down to the cut, the figure is about $79 per new client. This is the 100% attribution view below.

Q4 2026 at $105,000

Marketing is currently running at about $22,000 a quarter. Spending $105,000 in Q4 2026 adds about $83,000. Without it, expect about 1,300 new clients, in line with Q3.

For scale, Q4 2025 spent $103,425 and brought 1,964 new clients in total. The model's 2,060 is close to that. The 100% view's 2,340 would be the best quarter yet, so treat it as the upper end.

Spending $2,000 to $5,000 a month per studio

Each column is one quarter at that monthly spend, and what the new clients it brings spend in their first nine months. At the model's $108 per new client, $2,000 a month brings about 56 new clients a quarter to a studio, and $5,000 a month about 139.

Uses the model's $108 per new client in every studio, and each studio's own nine-month value per new client. There is not enough data to measure how each studio responds separately, and returns may fall at higher spend.

The April price increase

Existing members were repriced in April 2026: the Mighty Monthly Pass went from $295 to $330 in the East Bay and LA, and from $340 to $360 in SF and Marin. Student and Founders passes did not change. Drop-in, pack and new-member prices had already risen 12–18% in January.

Some members leave every month whatever the price, so the measure is attrition above the normal rate. I followed the 387 members paying the old price in February 2026. The baseline is members on the same pass in February 2025, when there was no increase, matched on how long each had been a member and which price they paid.

Net effect on monthly billings from repriced members

The price lift arrives in full in April. The extra cancellations build up after it.
  • August and September together averaged a $4,500 monthly loss. At that pace the $16,900 gain is used up around January; at September's pace, by November.
  • Members of two years or more stayed at the same rate as before (70%). Members between three months and two years dropped 11 to 18 points against the baseline.
