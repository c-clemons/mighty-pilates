## R3 · Page 3 — add four rows to the table

Insert after the existing "Customer identity" row, matching the current row style.
Replace the existing "Customer identity" body copy with the first row below.

| Label | Body copy |
|---|---|
| **Customer identity** | The studio management system issues a separate customer ID per location, so one person visiting three studios exists as three records. Identities are resolved across studios by email, then name. Using the raw IDs would overstate distinct customers by roughly 16%. |
| **Identity — known limits** | The match is a heuristic and fails in both directions. Someone who used different email addresses at different studios stays split, overstating the customer count and understating their individual lifetime value; a household sharing one email merges into a single customer, which does the reverse. About 84% of identities map to a single source record and only 28 map to seven or more, so aggregates hold — but no individual customer figure is exact. |
| **Non-customer records removed** | Front-desk staff create placeholder profiles for walk-ins and comps, and each studio has its own system login. Left in, these behave as single customers with hundreds of purchases and distort every per-customer average. 24 such identities and approximately $99,000 of associated sales are excluded. |
| **Duplicate records corrected** | When Marin was split out from Presidio Heights, sales before 2025-04-24 were written under both studios — 1,272 records and approximately $282,000 of double-counted sales. These are excluded, matching the treatment in the revenue recognition model that produces the general ledger. |

- **Do not relabel "net sales" as "revenue"** anywhere, including chart titles and
  axis labels. These are cash collections, not recognised revenue; they will not
  tie to the P&L, and a lender who tries to reconcile them will lose confidence in
  the packet.
- **Do not add revenue totals.** Their absence is deliberate.
- **Do not fill empty cells** in the page 5 cohort table. Blank means the cohort is
  not yet old enough to have that number. Zero would be wrong.
- **Do not remove or soften any DATA LIMITATION footnote**, including the existing
  ones on pages 4, 5 and 12.
- **Do not recompute or round-trip any figure.** If a number looks wrong, ask for a
  regenerated workbook rather than editing it.
- **Page 5 needs no mix caveat.** Cohort retention is genuinely flat across the
  period (month 12 is 13.7% for 2021 Q1 and 15.7% for 2025 Q1). That is a
  favourable finding and should not be hedged.
